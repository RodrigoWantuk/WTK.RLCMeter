## Intended change and rationale

The [DC finding](RGS-DC-Bias-Bench-Finding.md) identifies selected-driver current
through RGS into the analog source node. Retain the same 10 kOhm resistor and
existing driver/MOSFET pair, but change RGS from gate-to-common-source to
gate-to-GND. The selected driver's resistor load then returns to GND instead of
through RREF. When the driver turns off, the resistor still provides a gate
discharge path.

This candidate uses existing components and hookup wire. The first experiment is
limited to RGS4 / the 10K branch. Other branches remain unchanged and unqualified.
The source-end of the **resistor** must be isolated from its original PCB pad;
grounding that original source pad would short the analog node and is incorrect.
Do not remove the resistor and leave the MOSFET gate floating.

## Static checks and limitations

For a nominal driver output of 5 V, RGATE = 100 Ohm and a 10 kOhm gate-to-GND
pull-down, the calculated gate voltage is 4.95 V, driver load 0.495 mA and resistor
dissipation 2.45 mW. These are nominal circuit calculations, not physical data.
They remove the intentional RGS-to-source DC path in the ideal model. They do not
remove MOSFET leakage, parasitic capacitance, switch resistance, gate coupling or
driver supply noise.

The off-state argument is restricted to the intended nonnegative low-voltage AFE
domain: gate near GND gives nonpositive VGS for a nonnegative common source.
Selected VGS remains gate voltage minus actual source voltage, not 5 V. Negative
source excursions, power sequencing and transient gate behavior require explicit
measurements before wider use. Ground connection failure is also a gate-off fault.
Do not equate these calculations with safety qualification.

  DS11208 Rev.23-2: driver type and electrical characteristics. No low-current

## Product impact and changed off-state reference

This candidate does not inherently require new RREF values, removal of a range,
changes to the central impedance equation or replacement of the MCU/AFE/relays.
Its purpose is to restore a usable signal, not to prove final accuracy. Actual
supported range/frequency/amplitude combinations still depend on characterization
and calibration after rework. No existing hardware-specific calibration should
be assumed valid across this analog topology change.

The approximately 0.5 mA calculated above is the selected resistor's total driver
load, not an additional 0.5 mA relative to every original range, and not six times
that current with exclusive selection. The original RGS already consumed current;
the difference depends on source voltage. It is not a battery-runtime prediction.

The important tradeoff is off-state referencing. Original RGS pulls gate toward
source; the candidate pulls gate toward board GND. For a 1.65 V source and gate
near zero, VGS is approximately -1.65 V, consistent with an enhancement NMOS off.
If the source goes negative, VGS can become positive even with the gate at GND;
sufficient excursion can cause unwanted conduction. This must be checked during
switching, inductive transients and power collapse rather than assuming that a
gate-to-GND pull-down guarantees off in every condition. Reset/charger K1 logic
is untouched, but qualification of the range bank must be repeated.

The relevant primary principle is gate-to-source control and the role of a
source-referenced fault pull-down, as described in
[Nexperia AN90059, sections 1 and 6](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/application-note/AN90059.pdf).
This reference does not validate this particular candidate or purchased MOSFETs.

The existing-board wire connection must be short, mechanically secured and kept
away from sensitive analog nodes. A subsequent production PCB should route the
reviewed connection directly and record the hardware variant; hand rework is not
a substitute for manufacturing/reliability validation.
